## Overview

The United States and China agreed to establish a bilateral communication channel for AI-related incidents following the September 23-25, 2026 Washington summit between President Donald Trump and President Xi Jinping.

The mechanism is intended to provide a way for both governments to communicate about AI-related incidents and associated risks and benefits. Both countries also agreed to establish a broader China-U.S. AI Dialogue, with the next exchange scheduled for November 2026.

## Technical Details

Public information does not yet specify the technical architecture or operating procedures of the channel.

- The channel is not described as a shared AI platform.
- The channel is not described as a joint AI-development system.
- The current agreement establishes a government-to-government communication mechanism for AI incidents.

Earlier September discussions reportedly included possible cooperation on monitoring AI-directed cyberattacks and information sharing between AI laboratories, but these proposals should not be treated as confirmed capabilities of the newly announced channel.

## Attack Scenario

No specific attack triggered creation of the channel.

A potential scenario could involve an AI-related incident with cross-border or national-security implications, such as:

1. An AI-assisted cyberattack affecting cross-border interests.
2. Uncontrolled AI-enabled activity with escalation risk.
3. An incident involving AI systems in sensitive military or critical-infrastructure contexts.
4. One government notifying the other through the channel.
5. Clarification and exchange of relevant incident information.
6. De-escalation steps to reduce misinterpretation or crisis amplification.

## Impact Assessment

=== "Primary Impact"

    - Establishes a formal U.S.-China mechanism for potential AI-related crisis communication
    - May improve speed and clarity of bilateral incident notification

=== "Strategic and Operational Impact"

    - Could reduce uncertainty and misinterpretation during serious AI events
    - Provides a structured path for communication without requiring technology integration

=== "Limitations"

    - Announcement does not establish common AI-security standards
    - Announcement does not establish joint AI development
    - Announcement does not establish automatic information sharing
    - U.S. statements indicate continued independent AI development

## Mitigation Strategies

1. Establish clear criteria defining what constitutes an AI incident.
2. Create 24/7 government contact points for urgent coordination.
3. Develop incident-severity tiers and escalation procedures.
4. Establish secure information-sharing mechanisms.
5. Coordinate AI-related cyber-incident response procedures.
6. Develop methods for verifying incident reports before escalation.
7. Conduct bilateral tabletop exercises for AI-incident scenarios.
8. Define information-classification and releasability rules.
9. Establish dedicated procedures for AI-enabled military or critical-infrastructure incidents.
